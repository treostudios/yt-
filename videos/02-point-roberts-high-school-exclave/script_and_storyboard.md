# 02 · The Point Roberts High School Exclave

**Editorial premise:** The school bus leaves the United States, comes back into the United States, and then does both again on the ride home. Show why that is true instead of adding a second story. **Target cut:** 26 seconds, 9:16, 30 fps. [Frame timeline](timeline.json) · [Timed captions](captions.srt) · [Upload pack](upload_pack.md)

## Retention and accuracy audit

| Draft line or move | What breaks | Editorial fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Clear armed international customs four times every single day” | A mouthful before the viewer knows what to look at. “Armed” sells danger the story doesn't deliver; “every single day” implies weekends. | Name the school bus first. Count crossings, not inspections. The school context and return trip make the cadence clear. |
| “Welcome to … accidentally stranded south of Canada” | Greeting stalls the second beat; “stranded” and “accidentally” editorialize before the geography makes sense. | Reveal Point Roberts on the map with the contradiction: school and home are both in Washington. |
| “Diplomats … without looking at an actual coastline map” | Unsupported claim about intent and knowledge. The 1846 treaty extended an existing latitude boundary westward and specified a channel turn, not an infinitely straight border. | Keep the year and show what that boundary does at this peninsula. |
| “Trapping an American town with zero roads” | Sounds as if Point Roberts has no roads. What it lacks is a road connection to the rest of the U.S. that stays inside the U.S. | Show the Canadian road connection. The route supplies the proof, not another sentence about isolation. |
| Milk, doctor, passports, diplomats | Unnecessary detour; falsely makes local errands sound impossible and implies US–Canada land crossings routinely produce passport stamps. | Save screen time for the school commute. SFX mark crossings, not stamped documents. |
| “Virtually crime-free,” robbery joke, and “only people bold enough” | Unsourced absolute, causal leap, and a closing joke that breaks the setup–payoff chain. | Give crossings three and four their own visible beats; end on the bus again, not a different subject. |

## Locked draft narration

> This school bus crosses an international border four times a day.
>
> And the school is in the same state.
>
> In 1846, the Oregon Treaty extended the border along the forty-ninth parallel.
>
> That put the tip of this peninsula in Washington.
>
> From Point Roberts, the bus goes into Canada, then back into the U.S. at Blaine.
>
> After school? Same trip in reverse.
>
> All for a school in Washington.

**Voice direction:** Neutral North American English, low-mid natural register, crisp consonants, conversational rather than announcer-heavy. Start straight into “This school bus”; put weight on **FOUR** and **SAME STATE**, not “international.” Read “in eighteen forty-six” as a run-up to **FORTY-NINTH**. Leave a short pocket after “Canada,” step down on “Blaine” (rhymes with “rain”), and give “After school?” a quick lift. The final “Washington” lands dry; no end-of-video drawl. Start at roughly 165 WPM and use the scratch read to check the caption onsets before locking VO. Judge pronunciation, natural phrasing, intelligibility, and delivery; accent is a creative brief, not a proxy for where YouTube will distribute the video.

## Cartography and motion handoff

- **Source layers:** Import the [IBC digital boundary](https://www.internationalboundarycommission.org/en/maps-coordinates/coordinates.php) as the mapping overlay, paired with a consistent cross-border shoreline and roads extract (e.g. OSM; retain [attribution](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright)). Start with a single cross-border DEM such as Copernicus GLO-30 to avoid a vertical datum seam. Record all layer versions, licenses and attributions in the render manifest or upload credits before publish. For the local working scene use a common CRS such as NAD83 / UTM 10N (EPSG:26910); compensate for grid convergence so camera orientation follows true north. Check coast/border/road registration in the final comp, not just in GIS.
- **Grounded route:** Public reporting verifies the trip through Canada to Blaine, not the district bus’s exact road sequence or port/lane. Boundary Bay is the Point Roberts land access (Tyee Drive ↔ 56 Street). Pacific Highway (BC-15 ↔ WA SR-543) is a candidate to check with Blaine School District transportation; generic car directions via Peace Arch are **not** proof of a school-bus itinerary. Pin the school to the district-listed campus at 1055 H Street. If transport confirms a public corridor, snap each leg to actual connected road centerlines, QA the border intersections and approach direction, and drive the bus from arc length + tangent on the draped road spline. Compress travel time only; never simplify across the bay, paint an impossible turn, or show student pickup points, precise schedules, or inspection lanes. If the return leg differs, build it independently rather than reversing the path data.
- **Fallback if not verified:** Keep the geographically real base, opening bus on the public approach, and verified origin/destination pins. In the wide shots use sequential highlights on the two country fills and endpoint markers (`USA → CANADA → USA`), no connecting trace or speculative Blaine port dot, and caption a compact `ROUTE SHOWN SCHEMATICALLY` disclosure above the map labels. Preserve cues 1–4 as country-transition ticks; don’t animate a bus on the unconfirmed corridor. Resume the bus only for the closing match move.
- **Terrain/light:** Deep teal ocean center `#153D48`, navy edges `#091A28`, Canada `#6D8872`, Washington `#B99E6F`; amber bus/route `#FFC84B`, warm white border `#F2EDE3`. Begin with 1.15× relief, NW light (azimuth 315°, elevation 40°), clipped hillshade, and a broad low-opacity contact shadow under coast edges. Keep the Fraser Delta, Boundary Bay flats and Point Roberts visually low; use a coastal shadow, not a cliff wall. No tall extrusion for geopolitics. Use a darker outline under border and route; start at 4 px border and 6 px route at 1080 wide, then validate on mobile. Bus touches the terrain visually: small contact shadow, orientation from the road tangent, no bob or overshoot at turns.
- **Motion and flag cards:** Animate one persistent orthographic camera rig; scale in eased/log space with matching coast anchors between scenes. Keep key labels screen-facing, collision-managed and independent of terrain depth. Rounded flag cards are secondary, with minimally drawn eyes; optional Canadian counterpart appears only after crossing 1 if it does not compete with the count or labels. One glance/blink, no dialogue, bounce, or flag emoji. Main composition needs to read without avatars.
- **Subtitles:** Use the timed captions as the scratch edit, then adjust against the recorded performance. Purposefully phrase-break at stressed words, not every word individually. Center at roughly x=505, y=1350, with two lines max, inside x=105–900 and y=280–1500; keep key cartography above the caption band. Start at 60 px semibold, 5–6 px dark outline, soft shadow, white text and limited amber emphasis. Verify on actual Shorts UI; uploader overlays vary. Keep the transcript and burned captions synchronized, and avoid double-stacked auto-captions during QA.
- **Mix:** Separate VO, map FX, and environmental stems at 48 kHz. Start with a gentle HPF near 80 Hz, light de-essing and ~2–4 dB of compression on the VO. Use soft tire/engine bed and light coastal air; roll off unhelpful sub-bass so the rumble reads on a phone. Duck the bed and whooshes under syllables; put mechanical ticks in small VO gaps where possible. No gun-like hits or literal passport-stamp graphic. Print near −14 LUFS integrated with ≤ −1 dBTP as working delivery targets, check mono, and smooth the bed seam with a short wraparound overlap using source handles (not by adding a fade to black or silence).
