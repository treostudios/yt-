## 55-second scratch read and visual beats

| Window | Voiceover | Editorial and visual beat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:05 | “This school bus leaves the country just to take kids to high school. And the school is in the same state.” | On a moving bus at frame zero. Land `SAME STATE` over matching Washington pins. The bus approaches Boundary Bay. |
| 0:05–0:12 | “It starts in Point Roberts, Washington. The only road out of town heads north, straight into Canada.” | Show crossing **1** by ~0:08 on the public Boundary Bay approach. Reveal British Columbia in the same camera move. |
| 0:12–0:21 | “That’s Blaine, right across Boundary Bay. It’s right there. Unfortunately, the bus is not a boat.” | Hold both endpoints; leave a half-beat on the water before “Unfortunately.” Deliver the line deadpan. Keep the road explanation visual: highlight the landward exit, with no fake route across the bay. A soft whoosh hands off to quiet coastal air. |
| 0:21–0:30 | “Why is this tip even American? In 1846, the Oregon Treaty extended the border along the forty-ninth parallel. That left the tip on the U.S. side.” | Walk the border across the peninsula. Dry pencil texture and a single soft impact at the real boundary; don't imply a historical map-reading error. |
| 0:30–0:42 | “So the bus goes through British Columbia, crosses back into the U.S. at Blaine, and pulls up to high school. That’s about forty minutes on the bus. One way.” | Crossing **2** around 0:33; land `~40 MIN ONE WAY` after the school pin. Keep it an estimate, not a timetable. As “One way” lands, swap the map label for a stylized, fictional late slip: `REASON FOR TARDINESS: CANADA.` Hold for ~1.5 seconds, then cut to the last bell and ride home. No VO over the slip, no laugh sting, and no real student name or claim that the kids arrive late. |
| 0:42–0:55 | “Then the last bell rings, and they do it all again. Into Canada. Back into Washington. Four border crossings, just to go to school in their own district.” | Crossings **3** and **4** around 0:46 and 0:50. On the last line use school-district naming over the two Washington pins, not a continuous fill through Canada. Keep the bed running as the bus match-wipes to the next school morning. |

## Decision gate

- Watch the scratch cut once muted. By ~0:08 the viewer should have seen crossing 1 and should know that both endpoints are in Washington. If not, repair the opening before judging the runtime.
- Watch the 0:12–0:30 middle back to back with the short cut. If the bay reveal and the treaty beat feel like the same answer twice, trim that middle rather than artificially filling 55 seconds.
- Keep the hook and fourth crossing free of jokes. If either gag slows the first rough cut, drop the boat line first and keep the late slip if it reads clearly on a phone.
- Publish only one cut. Compare Video #1 at equal post-publish ages using stayed to watch, average view duration, APV, and retention around crossing 1, the treaty, arrival, and crossing 4. At 55 seconds, >120% APV requires >66 seconds of average view duration. Judge the longer cut on absolute watch time and late retention too, rather than expecting the same replay behavior as a 26-second Short. Country mix and revenue cannot be inferred from public competitor views.
