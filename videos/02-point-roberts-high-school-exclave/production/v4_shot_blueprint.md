# The Border Bus · V4 creative rebuild

**Production target:** 53.0 seconds · 1080 × 1920 · 60 fps CFR · 48 kHz audio. This is a new script and animation blueprint, not a voice recording or rendered master. The V3 MP4 stays intact. [Frame cue sheet](v4_sync_cues.json)

## What changes from V3

V3 used storyboard frames for important pointers. For example, the “right there” pulse was scheduled separately from the word onset; a pin could be visible and still fail to react on the word. V4 locks the dry voice track first and uses one measured marker to drive the feature, subtitle emphasis and sound. **The first video frame must already change on the cue**: a complete bright core on frame 0 of the effect, then a growing ring or glow. A slow fade beginning at the word would still read as late.

The new script opens with **“School bus. Via Canada.”** Before the viewer knows the town's name, they know the conflict. The story then moves from a seemingly obvious shortcut to the treaty that made it impossible by road, then follows the cost in minutes and crossings. The joke is the letdown at the end of the journey, not a random gag thrown over the map.

## VO lock · read only the words in bold

`/` = 0.10–0.16 s pocket; `//` = 0.22–0.35 s beat; `///` = ~0.36 s setup pause. Bracketed directions aren’t spoken. Read “1846” as “eighteen forty-six.” Use the same V3 narrator identity, the previously assigned `voice-01`: warm General American documentary baritone, unhurried consonants, no hype, no smile on the punchline. Reuse the voice identity, **not** V3’s audio, which has different words.

1. **School bus.** / **Via Canada.** [“Canada” lands in the first 1.5 s.]
2. **Both ends are in Washington.**
3. **Point Roberts is right here.** / **Blaine High is right there.** [Stress **here** and **there**, not “right.”]
4. **Eleven miles across the bay.** / **Nice shortcut.** // **Shame there’s no bridge.**
5. **Why does Washington have a piece over here?**
6. **In 1846, the Oregon Treaty extended the border along the forty-ninth parallel.**
7. **It put this tip in the U.S.** / **The only road out leads into Canada.**
8. **So the bus crosses here, loops around the bay, and crosses back at Blaine.** / **Then it pulls up to school.**
9. **Two countries.** / **About forty minutes.** /// **And the prize is algebra.** [Leave ~0.36 s before “And”; say “algebra” flat, then leave ~0.45 s without narration.]
10. **After school?** / **Canada again.** / **Then Washington.** // **Four border crossings.** / **Same school district.**

**Comment-magnet beat:** On “the prize,” a little award card starts to unfold from the campus pin. On the **first vowel of “algebra”**, the foil face flips to a worksheet: `SOLVE FOR x`. The map and bus stay visible; one paper click at the flip, no laugh sting. If this is the only gag the audience remembers, it still sums up the irony of the story. Optional pinned comment, not VO: **“School commute or international field trip with homework?”**

## Director’s implementation rules

- **Conform, don't guess.** Record or synthesize six short matched sections, assemble the dry 48 kHz master, then mark the actual onset of every named stressed syllable in the waveform. Use the audible first vowel of **“here”** or **“there”** after the preceding consonant opens, not the caption start. Store source dialogue sample, revised output frame and scene object ID. Targets are not approval. Once marked, do not retime VO separately from the visual or sound event tracks.
- **Hit policy at 60 fps.** A delivery frame lasts 16.67 ms. Snap each primary hit to the nearest presented frame (at most ~8.34 ms from measured onset before playback effects); sound design can land at the exact 48 kHz sample. If a visual cannot arrive late at all, choose the frame containing or preceding the onset instead and log the small lead. Export and inspect the actual MP4 around the hit rather than promising literal millisecond accuracy from an unrecorded script. One-frame bright core with 8–14 frames of decay; no repeated screen-wide flashes.
- **One geographic event, four consumers.** For the four crossings, solve each mapped route/border intersection by cumulative chainage. Retime the bus distance curve so the front axle hits that sample on the conformed word marker. The same event drives the bus, border glow, badge and sound tick. Use separate outbound/return centerlines, headings from tangents, and one small stop/roll anticipation. Don't use a linear lon/lat chase or a cross-dissolve to hide geographic jumps.
- **Terrain and lens.** One registered terrain rig: real available elevation for the local heightfield, matching map vectors, separate draped road/border layers, realistic low coastal relief, NW directional contact shadows, deep navy/teal ocean. Use shared camera tangents on the local→regional→local moves; no hard zoom on top of the “here” flash. No intro-only orbital fly-through: the overview is already present at frame zero in a locator and expands within the same geography. Prioritize an immediately readable bus over an empty globe.
- **Safety and credibility.** `≈11 MI / 18 KM` is the [V3 measured **shore-to-shore** distance](v3_motion_and_source_notes.md), not a navigable line, an operating ferry, or a road distance. Use the sourced road geometry as a **labeled example drive**, not an assertion that the district bus uses Pacific Highway, those lanes, or a specific campus driveway. Put the campus pin on the school and animate arrival only to the sourced public road. “About forty minutes” refers to the reported commute, not simulation time. Retain source credits and the on-screen disclosure. [Blaine High School](https://www.blainesd.org/o/blaine-high-school/) · [Commute reporting](https://archive.seattletimes.com/archive/?date=20050613&slug=pointroberts13m) · [Archived public road data](route_data/README.md) · [Boundary reference](https://www.internationalboundarycommission.org/en/maps-coordinates/coordinates.php)
- **Phone and mix QA.** Keep text within safe center, with 56–64 px subtitles, outlined high contrast, and map-object labels above them; test on the actual Shorts UI. Use the same dry voice, a light coastal/tire bed and a compact, mono-safe crossing tick. Inspect frames on both sides of every named marker, then review at normal speed and muted. Test the primary question cold: by 0:03.65, can the viewer identify that a trip with two Washington endpoints goes through Canada? By payoff, can they count all four? Recut if the answers aren’t clear; no claim that a script can guarantee completion.
